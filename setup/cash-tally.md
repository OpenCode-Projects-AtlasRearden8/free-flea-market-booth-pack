# Cash Tally Log — setup

**Prints:** 1 page, US Letter, portrait. Sales table plus end-of-day count-up.

## What it is

A one-sheet sales log: one line per sale, tally columns for cash versus
card/QR, a running total, and a block at the bottom that turns your counted
cash into an actual profit number.

## How to use it

1. **Put a pen and the log in the cash box.** Under the money, not on the
   table — a table with a log on it invites narration of every sale.
2. **Write every line, even the slow ones.** The running total is what keeps
   you honest when you count at 3 p.m.
3. **Note the human stuff.** "Held for J. back by 1", "took $6 on $8 after
   haggle", "gave free scarf with purchase" — this is the memory the sheet
   gives you at the next market.
4. **End-of-day count-up is built in.** Count at the booth (before packing),
   subtract the starting float, and check it against your total from the log.
   A small difference in coins is normal; a difference in the $5-and-up
   region means a miscount or a missing entry, not theft at your own table.
5. **Costs matter too.** Fuel, parking, coffee, a new tarp — write them in the
   expenses line so "landed profit" is the honest kind.
6. **Security rules are part of the sheet.** Nothing over $20 stays loose in
   an open till, bag under your chair, and never count money with a shopper
   leaning over the table.

## Pairings

- Pairs with the **Change-Counting Card** (the float) and the **"Sold" Ticket
  Stubs** (write a stub, log the same line).
- Feeds the "what sold 100%" line of the **Load-In / Load-Out Sheet**.

## Timing

One sheet per market day. File it with last week's — after three markets it
becomes the most useful spreadsheet you never had to build.
