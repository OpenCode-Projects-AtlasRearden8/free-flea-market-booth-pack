# Change-Counting Card — setup

**Prints:** 1 page, US Letter, portrait.

## What it is

A float planner and a cheat sheet in one: how much of each bill and coin to
bring, what each quantity counts up to, a scratch space for till arithmetic,
and quick-change formulas to whisper when a $20 shows up.

## How to use it

1. **Decide your float before 9 p.m. the night before.** Most first booths do
   fine with $60–$80: about one roll of quarters, half a roll each of dimes
   and nickels, a fistful of pennies, then $1 bills, $5s, $10s, and one $20.
2. **Fill the "value" blanks as you bag the change.** The row math is written
   on the sheet ($10 in dimes = one roll and a half, and so on) so banded
   rolls stop being guesswork.
3. **Bring rolls if you can.** Two rolls of quarters beats forty loose ones
   in every scenario except change-making.
4. **Scratch space is for the panic moment.** A "$8 item paid $20" moment is
   where hands shake; write the problem down, do the subtraction on paper,
   count money into the buyer's palm, say the change out loud.
5. **Keep the card in the cash box lid.** If the arithmetic is inside the lid,
   it is inside the transaction instead of lying on the table.

## Pairings

- The float total goes in the **Cash Tally Log** ("Minus starting float").
- The CASH ONLY sign on the **Big Readable Price Signs** page is why this
  card exists.

## Timing

Fill it in at the same table session where you pack — the money bag and the
price stickers end up built in the same ten minutes.
