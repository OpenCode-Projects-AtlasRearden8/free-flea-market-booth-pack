# Price Stickers & Tag Strips — setup

**Prints:** 3 pages, US Letter, portrait. Pages 1–2: round stickers at tag
size (24 per page). Page 3: cut-apart tag strips with item / size / price.

## What it is

Pricing labels sized for hanging tags and small items. Round stickers for the
five most common booth prices (50¢, $1, $2, $3) plus blanks for odd prices,
and rectangle tag strips for clothes and boxed goods.

## How to use it

1. **Print page 1 once a season.** $1 stickers sell more than you expect.
2. **Sticker or tape, not marker-on-the-neck.** A dot sticker at the ribline of
   a shirt or on the corner of a mug reads better and does not obliterate the
   actual brand tag (which shoppers use for sizing).
3. **Round your prices.** Odd prices like $3.75 earn pennies and cost minutes.
   Nobody haggles over quarters — get to $3 or $4 and be done.
4. **Use blanks when the price is unusual.** Fill the blank line with the
   price and, optionally, why "$8 — solid wood".
5. **Tag strips for anything you would not want a sticker on.** Cut, punch one
   hole top-left, thread a short string loop. Fill out everything (item, size,
   price) at home; hanging tags are what keep prices intact after item 40.
6. **Sold-out? Sticker-peel the item before it goes in the bag** so your
   remaining stock count stays honest against the Cash Tally Log.

## Pairings

- Prices match the **Big Readable Price Signs** language ($1 table = every
  item stickered $1).
- Anything priced above $10: write the price twice — sticker plus tag strip —
  so haggles have a starting point.

## Timing

Sticker everything at home, in the packing pile, the day before load-in. About
ten seconds per item.
