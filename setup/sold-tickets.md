# "Sold" Ticket Stubs — setup

**Prints:** 2 pages, US Letter, portrait. Eight twin-state stubs per page:
SOLD stubs and one HELD stub per sheet for items someone is returning to buy.

## What it is

Ticket stubs for the two moments every booth has to handle: the sale itself,
and the "I'll be back for it" hold. The dotted line marks where the stub
splits; the bottom half is the buyer's pickup promise, the top half stays with
the item.

## How to use it

1. **Print one or two pages per market.** Eight stubs per page covers most
   small-booth days; print two sheets if it is a big fair.
2. **Write at the moment of sale, not after.** Item, price, paid — three
   short lines. Fold or cut on the dotted line and hand the lower half over.
3. **The stub is the pickup pass.** "Item stays with the booth until this
   half comes back" is printed right on it — a named shopper who has paid
   gets it back without a photo of their hands, just a stub.
4. **The HELD stub is for the coming-back moment.** Item, agreed price, back
   by a time. Agree a real time ("back by 1 p.m." not "later") or the hold
   becomes storage. If they miss it, set the item back out; no resentment.
5. **Log it twice.** The same minute between the stub and the **Cash Tally
   Log** keeps your count and your hands in the same world.
6. **Fold the stub pad around the pen.** Sold-stub pad + pencil tucked in the
   till box means the sale gets recorded even in the busy season.

## Pairings

- Weds directly with the **Cash Tally Log**.
- Held items that never come back get their sticker peeled and the price
  sign language from **Big Readable Price Signs** takes over.

## Timing

Cut the pages into pads the night before, or just fold the sheet along the
gutters and carry it whole — the dotted line does the splitting in the booth.
